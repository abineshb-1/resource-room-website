The Program

Early intervention that begins
where your child is.

Small groups. Specialist-led. Hands-on.
Built around each child's unique learning profile.

The earlier the signs are met with the right support,
the less ground a child loses and the more confidence they carry forward.

4–5 children per group
Hands-on activity-based every session
Specialist led and observing throughout
Three stages

Children enter at their own level.
Move at their own pace.

Not age alone. Current level determines the starting point.
Progression between stages is natural as skills build.

Ages 3–4
Stage 01

Language and Play

The foundation all other learning grows from.

Oral language Expressive communication Listening & attention Vocabulary Social confidence Turn-taking
For

Children showing early signs of language delay or limited expressive communication.

Ages 4–6
Stage 02

Early Foundations

The most important window — where intervention has the greatest impact.

Sustained attention Fine motor Pencil grip Sequencing Phonological awareness Numeracy readiness Visual processing
For

Children showing early difficulty with letters, numbers, attention or coordination.

Ages 6–8
Stage 03

Learning Support

Rebuilding confidence alongside skills — structured, documented, parent-partnered.

Reading Phonological skills Written expression Number sense Working memory Self-regulation Learning confidence
For

Children with identified or emerging learning differences needing consistent, specialist support.

No child is labelled here. Every child is seen.

Parent partnership — always

A typical session

What happens inside
a Learnnest session.

1

Arrive and settle

Small, calm, familiar space. 3–4 other children. Specialist already present and ready.

2

Movement first

A physical warm-up — coordination, rhythm, movement path. Regulates the body so the mind can focus.

3

Core activity

Multisensory and hands-on — tracing, building, sequencing, numbers through objects. Designed to feel like play. Specialist observing throughout.

4

Reflection

Brief and without pressure — what felt good, what felt hard. A safe, honest moment for a child who has learned to stay quiet about difficulty.

5

Parent update

Regular notes in plain language — what your child is building, what to continue at home. A child's learning doesn't begin or end in a session.
