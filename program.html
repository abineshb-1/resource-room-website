The Program

Early intervention that begins
where your child is.

Small groups. Specialist-led. Hands-on.
Built around each child's unique learning profile.

Learning differences do not appear overnight. The early signs are there — in the way a child approaches letters, numbers, sequences, expression. The earlier those signs are met with the right intervention, the less ground a child loses and the more confidence they carry forward.

Learnnest works in that early window — with children aged 3 to 8 — building the foundational skills that make everything else possible.

Every session is designed.
Every child is observed.
Every family is kept close.

Small specialist groups

Sessions run in groups of 4 to 5 children — small enough for every child to be seen, supported and challenged at their own level.

Hands-on and activity-based

Every session is built around doing — not sitting and receiving. Children learn through movement, making, sorting, building, storytelling and play. The activity is the lesson.

Observation at the centre

Every session the specialist is watching — not just teaching. That observation builds each child's individual learning profile over time.

Three stages. One continuous journey.

Children enter at the stage that fits their current level — not their age alone. Movement between stages happens naturally as skills build.

01
Stage 1 · Language and Play · Ages 3–4

Language is the foundation of all learning.

Before a child can read, write or make sense of numbers, they need a rich internal language — words for their world, ways to express what they think and feel, the ability to listen and respond.

Stage 1 builds that foundation through structured play, storytelling, movement and social interaction. Every activity is purposeful. Every session is observed.

What we are building

Oral language · expressive communication · listening and attention · vocabulary · early social confidence · turn-taking and group participation

Who this is for

Children aged 3 to 4 showing early signs of language delay, limited expressive communication, or who would benefit from a rich structured language environment before formal learning begins.

02
Stage 2 · Early Foundations · Ages 4–6

The most important window.

Between 4 and 6 the foundational skills for reading, writing and numeracy begin to form. For children with learning differences this is the most important window — the stage where early intervention has the greatest impact.

Stage 2 works directly on those foundations through hands-on activity designed by a specialist who knows exactly what to look for and what to build.

What we are building

Sustained attention · fine motor coordination · pencil grip and hand strength · sequencing and pattern recognition · early phonological awareness · numeracy readiness · visual and auditory processing

Who this is for

Children aged 4 to 6 showing early signs of learning differences — difficulty with letters, numbers, sequences, attention or coordination.

03
Stage 3 · Learning Support · Ages 6–8

Rebuilding confidence alongside skills.

Children in Stage 3 have often already experienced the gap between what they know they can do and what the classroom asks of them. Early intervention at this stage rebuilds confidence alongside skills.

Stage 3 provides structured, specialist-led intervention for children with identified learning differences. Sessions are consistent, documented and built around each child's individual profile. Parents are active partners throughout.

What we are building

Reading and phonological skills · spelling and written expression · numeracy concepts and number sense · working memory strategies · self-regulation · learning confidence

Who this is for

Children aged 6 to 8 with identified or emerging learning differences who need structured early intervention — consistent, small group, purposeful and parent-partnered.

What makes Learnnest different.

Montessori-informed

Every activity passes through a Montessori-informed design filter — hands first, one concept at a time, child has agency, error is part of the process.

SLD-aware from the ground up

Both Learnnest founders are trained in Specific Learning Differences. That awareness is present in every session — in how activities are designed, how children are observed, and how families are guided.

Neurodiversity as a starting point

Children with learning differences experience and process the world differently. Learnnest begins from that difference — not despite it. Every session is built around the child's strengths first.

Parent partnership — always

Parents receive regular updates — in plain language, not clinical terms — on what their child is building and what can be continued at home.

A typical Learnnest session.

A child arrives and settles into a familiar space — small, calm, unhurried. There are 3 to 4 other children. The specialist is already there.

Every session begins with movement. Something physical — a simple coordination activity, a rhythm game, a movement path. This is not warm-up for its own sake. For children who find learning effortful, regulating the body before asking the mind to focus makes everything that follows more possible.

Then the core work begins. It might be a multisensory activity — tracing, building, touching and saying at the same time — because when more than one sense is involved, learning holds better. It might be a sequencing task — pictures, sounds, patterns — building the ability to hold order in the mind, which is often where children with learning differences need the most gentle, consistent practice. It might be working with numbers through objects and movement before a single symbol is introduced — because for some children, quantity needs to be felt before it can be counted.

A parent watching might think — this looks like play. That is exactly right. The activity is designed to feel that way. Underneath it the specialist is observing closely — noticing which sounds a child confuses, how they hold a pencil, whether they lose their place under pressure, how they respond when something feels hard. These observations build quietly into a picture of each child — one that shapes every session that follows.

Toward the end the child reflects — briefly, without pressure. What felt good today. What felt hard. For a child who has learned to stay quiet about difficulty, this small moment of safe honesty matters more than it looks.

Parents receive a regular update — in plain language — on what their child is building and what they are noticing. Because a child's learning does not begin or end in a session.
